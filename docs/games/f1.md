# F1 25 – UDP telemetry

Implementation: `crates/sy-source-f1`. Source: EA's official specifications
"Data Output from F1 25" (v3, May 2025, packet format **2025**) and "Data
Output from F1 25: 2026 Season Pack" (v1.2, Sep 2026, packet format
**2026**), PDFs from the EA forum. They are EA's copyright and not in the
repository; put them into `telemetry_examples/F125/` (ignored by git) for
reference. The first version was written from a copy of EA's header in the
`f1-packets` repository (read only); every packet size and every field we
read has since been checked against the official PDFs. Each parser is also
checked against the packet sizes the specs state.

> **Status:** checked with sixteen recordings (Windows, Nov 2026): time
> trials with the F1 2025 car (Catalunya, Hungaroring in UDP format 2025,
> Spa with a switch from 2025 to 2026), the F1 26 car (Suzuka; Austria with
> track limits, flashbacks across the line and a restarted lap; Monaco with
> wall riding and corner cutting; Melbourne with a format switch mid-lap,
> and once restarted from the menu),
> F2 2026 (Jeddah, with a pause), F2 2025 (Austria), F2 2024 (Austria
> reversed), two races: five laps with the F1 World car (Brazil, standing
> start, flashback) and five laps with the F1 26 car (Shanghai, pit stop,
> pause), a Grand Prix weekend at Monza (practice, qualifying with a
> flying-lap start, a race with pit stop and crash restarted with "restart
> session", then a red flag, standing restart and safety car), and a race
> in Melbourne with a virtual safety car. Lap times, sectors, validity and
> invalidation reasons match the game's own lap history in all of them;
> the 2026 active aero and overtake values match what was driven.
> Recordings go into
> `telemetry_examples/F125/` (raw `.syraw` files, *Settings → Advanced →
> Keep raw recordings*); `crates/sy-capture/tests/f1_recordings.rs` replays them.

## Setting up the game

In F1 25: *Game Options → Settings → UDP Telemetry Settings* (PC: also in
`Documents\My Games\<game>\hardwaresettings\hardware_settings_config.xml`,
tag `<udp …>`)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| UDP Telemetry | On |
| UDP Broadcast Mode | Off |
| UDP IP Address | `127.0.0.1` when the game runs on the same PC (also under Proton); otherwise the address of the PC running Stintory |
| UDP Port | 20777 (the app's default; changeable under *Settings → Games → F1 25*, applied right away) |
| UDP Send Rate | 60 Hz (lower rates work, with fewer samples per lap) |
| UDP Format | 2026 or 2025 (switching while driving is fine: the session and the lap go on, with the channels of the format the session started with). The game can also send the older formats 2024 and 2023; Stintory doesn't read them and says so under *Settings → Games → F1 25* |
| Your Telemetry | Doesn't matter: "restricted" only hides your car's data from other players online |

**Consoles (PlayStation, Xbox) and other PCs:** the game sends the same
packets over the network. Enter the Stintory PC's address in the game, and
turn on *Accept data from other devices* in Stintory (it then listens on all
network interfaces; the firewall may ask). Nothing else is needed, so the
Linux app can record a console or a PC game too.

**Other telemetry apps:** only one program can listen on a port. Let
Stintory receive the packets and forward them (*Forward to other apps*,
e.g. `127.0.0.1:20778`), then point the other app at that port.

## What is recorded

- Only the player's car (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §5.2). Before a packet is spooled,
  every other car's entry in per-car arrays is zeroed, also the second
  player's in split screen. Packets about other cars (session history of
  others, tire sets, final classification, lobby, lap positions, time trial
  rival) are dropped, as are events that name a car (overtakes, collisions,
  other drivers' penalties, fastest lap, …). The player's own penalties are
  kept (without the other car involved): they say why a lap is invalid.
  Forwarded packets are not changed.
- Nothing while spectating (that's another driver's car), in the garage or
  in the menus before a time trial: a session only starts once the player
  drives.
- One sample per car telemetry packet. Canonical channels: speed, throttle,
  brake, clutch, `steer_norm` (the steering input −1…1; the game doesn't
  report the wheel angle), gear, rpm, position, g-forces, yaw, fuel (kg),
  brake bias. Extras `f1.*`: DRS, ERS store/deploy mode (0 none, 1 medium,
  2 hotlap, 3 overtake; "boost" in the 2026 spec)/deployed this lap,
  `f1.ers_power` (MGU-K power output, W), engine temperature, front wheel angle, per wheel tire surface and inner
  temperatures, brake temperatures, tire pressures, slip ratio, suspension
  position, the surface under each wheel (`f1.surface_rl` …: 0 tarmac,
  1 rumble strip, 2 concrete, 3 rock, 4 gravel, 5 mud, 6 sand, 7 grass,
  8 water, 9 cobblestone, 10 metal, 11 ridged); 2026 format also
  `f1.active_aero_mode` (0 corner mode, 1 straight mode) and
  `f1.overtake_active` (1 while overtake mode is used: available when
  close enough to the car ahead at the overtake marker).
- What a car doesn't have reads 0 throughout (checked with the recordings):
  the F1 26 cars report no DRS; F2 cars (2024–2026) have DRS but no ERS
  (store, mode and power 0); the cars before 2026 report active aero and
  overtake mode as 0 in the 2026 format. MGU-K power goes up to 120 kW
  before 2026 and above (315 kW seen) with the F1 26 cars; it is never
  negative (harvesting doesn't show). In time trial the ERS store stays
  full and overtake mode is on all lap with the F1 26 cars.
- The comparison shows ERS mode, power and store, DRS, straight mode and overtake
  under *More channels*, each only when one of the compared laps has a
  value other than 0 (so only for the cars that have it).
- The car setup of each lap (exported only when chosen; the recordings
  have setup changes within a session, e.g. the fuel load between runs) and
  a player-only session info:
  UDP format, session type, formula, track id and length, assists, game
  mode and rule set (with names), online or not, AI difficulty, team id,
  race number.
- Send rates (from the spec): motion, lap data, telemetry, status, extra
  motion and telemetry 2 at the rate chosen in the game, all in the same
  frame; session and car setups 2 per second; participants every 5 s; car
  damage 10 per second; session history 20 per second, cycling through the
  cars.

## Sessions, laps, validity

- A session is one `sessionUID` for the player's car index. In online
  sessions every driver has another index, so the ids of their sessions and
  laps differ. A new `sessionUID` or the event `SEND` ends the session.
  Each part of a weekend (practice, qualifying, race) has its own
  `sessionUID`, as does a race after *Restart session*: one recording can
  hold several sessions. Restarting a time trial from the menu also starts
  a new session.
  Keys: session `f1_25:<sessionUID hex>:<car index>`, track
  `f1_25:<trackId>`, car `f1_25:<teamId>/<formula>`.
- The session's wall-clock start is the receive time of its first packet
  minus its session time (stored with each packet in the spool, so a replay
  gives the same start).
- Lap times: when the lap number goes up, `lastLapTimeInMS` is the official
  time of the finished lap. Sectors 1 and 2 are the last values seen in that
  lap, sector 3 is the rest.
- A lap is invalid when `currentLapInvalid` was set **at the end** of the
  lap, or the player's session history clears its lap-valid bit. The state
  during the lap is not used, because a flashback can undo an invalidation.
- The reason comes from the player's penalty events (`PENA`, penalty types
  10–15 "lap invalidated"): corner cutting, running wide, wall riding,
  flashback used, reset to track. Some penalties also cost the next lap
  ("this and next lap invalidated") or the previous one. A flashback to
  before a penalty takes it back. In races the game often invalidates a lap
  with a different penalty (a warning, a stop-go) for a track-limits
  infringement; Stintory uses those when there is no "lap invalidated"
  penalty. Without either the reason is "invalidated by the game".
- Flashback (event `FLBK`): the samples from the flashback's session time on
  are removed, and the lap is flagged *flashback*. In time trial the game
  invalidates the lap right after a flashback.
- A flashback can go back across the line into the lap before (seen 7 s
  back from 1 s after the line). The game then times that lap again (and
  in time trial invalidates it). So Stintory holds a finished lap back for
  30 s of session time before it stores it; during a live recording a lap
  shows up that much later.
- **Restart lap** (time trial menu): the game puts the car up to about a
  kilometer before the line (Austria 1100 m, Spa 1300 m), sets the lap
  time to 0 and keeps the lap number. The attempt so far becomes an
  incomplete lap ("lap restarted") and its penalties no longer count; the
  run-up to the line isn't recorded; the lap counts again from the line,
  with the same number. Detected from the lap time going back while the
  session time goes on (a flashback sets both back).
- Before the line the game counts lap 1 with a negative `lapDistance`.
  - Time trial (and every session that isn't a race): that's the out-lap
    from the pit exit, from almost a lap (Catalunya: 36 m after the line)
    to 185 m (Austria reversed). Stintory makes it lap 0, flagged out-lap,
    with the distance from the line. An invalidation in the out-lap carries
    over into lap 1 (the game's history agrees).
  - Race: that's the grid (Brazil: 42 m before the line). The game's time
    for lap 1 starts at the start, so lap 1 keeps these samples.
- Some grids are after the line (Monza: 274 m, Shanghai: 15 m). A race lap
  that starts with the car standing still on the grid counts from there;
  otherwise a lap that starts further than 50 m from the line was joined
  mid-lap and is incomplete.
- **Red flag** (event `RDFL`): the lap is cut off ("red flag"), nothing is
  recorded until the race resumes. The game skips ahead (Monza: from 30 s
  to 1830 s of session time, lap 1 to lap 3) to a standing restart from
  the grid; that lap counts from the grid. The game's times of the laps it
  skipped aren't ours.
- The race's last lap: at the flag the game does **not** count up the lap
  number; only `lastLapTimeInMS` changes (and the result becomes
  "finished"). That ends the lap; the cool-down gets the next number.
- The game's "lap invalid" flag can already be set when a lap starts: a
  penalty "this and next lap invalidated" (seen after cutting the last
  chicane).
- Out-lap: the lap starts in the pit lane (`pitStatus` ≠ 0); in-lap: it ends
  there. Going back to the garage mid-lap ends the lap as incomplete.
- Outside races the game doesn't count the line in the pit lane: the lap
  number stays and the lap time starts again. Stintory ends the in-lap
  there (with its own measured time, the game has none); the way to the
  garage isn't a lap. An out-lap straight back into the pits is an out-
  and in-lap.
- Qualifying "flying lap" start: the car is put before the line (Monza:
  2100 m), like the time trial's out-lap: lap 0, flagged out-lap.
- **Pause:** the game sends nothing while paused, and its session time
  stands still. The recording stays open while a session runs (from its
  first packet until `SEND`) for up to an hour of silence; after a session
  ended, 30 s of silence end the recording. Quitting a session sends
  "paused" and then `SEND`. Lap times include no pause.

## Axes and signs (checked)

- World X and Z are horizontal, Y is up. `pos_x` = X, `pos_y` = −Z gives an
  unmirrored map: steering left (`steer_norm` < 0) turns the path
  counter-clockwise. Mapping Z directly mirrored the map.
- The game's lateral g is negative in right-hand turns; `g_lat` is
  positive there (as for every game).
- The game's yaw is the direction of travel from +Z towards +X; `yaw` is
  the heading clockwise from +`pos_y` (π − yaw).

## Names

Track, team, session type, game mode, rule set, surface and penalty names
come from the specs' appendices (`tables.rs`). Team ids are unique across
formulas and seasons; the car name is the team plus the series and the
season of the car, e.g. "McLaren (F1 2025)", "Mercedes (F1 26)", "Campos
(F2 2025)", "F1 Generic (F1 World)":

| Ids | Cars |
|---|---|
| 0–9 | F1 2025: Mercedes, Ferrari, Red Bull Racing, Williams, Aston Martin, Alpine, RB, Haas, McLaren, Sauber |
| 185–194 | F1 2024, same order |
| 476–486 | F1 26 (2026 cars), same order, then Audi, Cadillac |
| 158–168, 465–475, 489–499 | F2 2024 / 2025 / 2026: Art GP, Campos, Rodin Motorsport, AIX Racing, DAMS, Hitech, MP Motorsport, Prema, Trident, Van Amersfoort Racing, Invicta |
| 41, 104, 129/155, 142/154 | F1 Generic (the F1 World car), F1 Custom Team, Konnersport, APXGP |

Our recordings confirm 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 41, 160, 466, 476, 477, 479, 482,
486 and 499. Unknown ids show by
number ("team 600 (F2)"). Only names depend on these tables; keys use the
numbers.

## Reprocessing a recording

After a fix in the adapter, delete the session in the app and import the
raw `.syraw` file (*Import*, file type "Raw recording"): it is processed
again with the current code. Laps that are still stored are left as they
are. Several files chosen together are read as one recording in the order
they were recorded, so a session that an older version split in two (a
pause) comes back as one.

Penalty names are the game's: a cut across the grass came as "running
wide" (infringement 26), not as corner cutting; corner cutting (25) came
in Monaco. Which one the game gives seems to depend on the corner.

Safety car and virtual safety car need nothing special: the laps go on
as usual, just slower.
